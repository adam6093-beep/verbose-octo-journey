⚡ HUMAN METABOLISM & ATP

Detailed Concept Map & Study Notes

1. What is Metabolism?

Metabolism (all chemical reactions in your body) is the set of processes that convert nutrients into cellular energy (ATP) to keep you alive.

Two Main Pathways:
  • Anabolism (building up): Combines small, simple molecules into large, complex ones. Requires ATP energy
    Example: Glycogenesis (building stored sugar from individual glucose units) or making muscle protein.
  • Catabolism (breaking down): Breaks complex nutrients down into smaller pieces to release stored energy. Produces ATP energy
    Example: Glycolysis (splitting sugar molecules open).

2. The 3 Macromolecules

These are the three major nutrients (large food molecules) converted into ATP energy:

  • Carbohydrates: Broken down into Glucose (simple blood sugar).
    • Role: First-choice, fastest fuel source for cells. Essential for brain and red blood cells. (~4 kcal/g)
  • Lipids (Fats): Broken down into Free Fatty Acids + Glycerol.
    • Role: High-density, long-term energy storage reserves. (~9 kcal/g — most energy-dense)
  • Proteins: Broken down into Amino Acids (building blocks of muscle and tissue).
    • Role: Used for structure, enzymes, and repair. Used for energy only as a last resort during starvation. (~4 kcal/g)

3. Macromolecule Pools

The body maintains circulating nutrient pools (free nutrients in blood and fluids) that constantly exchange with storage deposits.

  • Glucose Pool: Managed tightly in blood.
    • Storage: Stored as Glycogen (branched sugar chains) in the Liver (for the body) and Skeletal Muscles (for muscle use only). Limited capacity!
  • Fatty Acid Pool:
    • Storage: Stored as Triglycerides in Adipose Tissue (fat cells). Virtually unlimited storage capacity.
  • Amino Acid Pool:
    • Storage: No true "storage pool." Excess amino acids cannot be saved; they are converted to glucose/fat or used for energy, losing nitrogen as Urea (urine waste).

4. ATP Pathways

The body uses 3 connected chemical stages to turn glucose into ATP cellular energy:

  • 1. Glycolysis (Sugar-Splitting):
    • Where: Cytoplasm (fluid inside cell).
    • Process: Breaks 1 Glucose into 2 Pyruvate molecules.
    • Yield: 2 ATP. (Does not require oxygen).
  • 2. Citric Acid Cycle / Krebs Cycle:
    • Where: Mitochondrial Matrix (center of power-plant organelle).
    • Process: Strips high-energy electrons off acetylated fuel.
    • Yield: 2 ATP, plus electron carriers (NADH & FADH₂). Releases CO₂ as waste.
  • 3. Electron Transport Chain:
    • Where: Inner Mitochondrial Membrane (inner wall of mitochondrion).
    • Process: Uses oxygen to harvest high-energy electrons (Oxidative Phosphorylation).
    • Yield: ~28 to 32 ATP (Massive energy payout!).

5. Absorptive vs. Post-Absorptive

How the body shifts processes based on whether food was recently eaten or not:

  • Absorptive State (Fed State):
    • Timing: During to ~4 hours after eating.
    • Key Hormone: Insulin (lowers blood sugar).
    • Goal: Use incoming glucose for ATP; store all excess nutrients (Glycogenesis & Lipogenesis). Anabolic state.
  • Post-Absorptive State (Fasting State):
    • Timing: Late between meals, overnight, or starvation.
    • Key Hormone: Glucagon (raises blood sugar) + Epinephrine.
    • Goal: Maintain blood sugar level. Breaks down stored glycogen (Glycogenolysis) or creates NEW glucose from amino acids (Gluconeogenesis). Catabolic state.
